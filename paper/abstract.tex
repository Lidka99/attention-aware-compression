\begin{abstract}
We measure how channel-width allocation affects the top-1 accuracy of
folded CNNs at matched parameter counts. We compare a uniform channel
reduction with a linear depth profile using the same folding operator and
batch-normalization recalibration, without post-compression weight
fine-tuning. On newly trained ResNet18 checkpoints, at nominal parameter
reductions of 30\%, 50\%, and 70\%, the linear profile improves top-1 over
uniform folding by 3.08--11.65 percentage points on CIFAR-100 and
3.31--9.49 points on Tiny ImageNet. All four paired checkpoints favor
the linear profile at each cut on both datasets. The profile uses
1.105--1.824 times as many multiply--accumulate operations (MACs) as
uniform folding. At comparable MAC counts, a wider uniform folded model
with more parameters achieves higher top-1 in 10 of 15 earlier development
settings. The results quantify an accuracy gain at a fixed parameter
budget and the arithmetic cost of that gain.
\end{abstract}
